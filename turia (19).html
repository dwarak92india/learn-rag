The unit that matters

Nothing attaches to an ingredient. Everything attaches to a form.

Evidence, regulation, specification and claims attach to a specific form — not to "ashwagandha." Public databases collapse forms. Inherit that collapse and everything downstream is quietly wrong until a regulator or a failed batch finds it.

A trial on one deposited probiotic strain substantiates nothing for another strain of the same species. Every regulator treats strain identity as binding.

Where a source names only the ingredient we mark it INGREDIENT_ONLY — scored weaker everywhere, never silently promoted.

The design space, before the bench.

Synthetic chemistry can be simulated; it has physics underneath. Formulation has a documented feasible region instead — and Matrix draws it.

Feasible region · magnesium bisglycinate
■ feasible   ■ feasible with a split dose or an engineered form   ■ does not fit

Every cell is arithmetic or a documented constraint — payload against salt mass, temperature against thermal tolerance, pH against pigment stability. Nothing simulated.

Change bisglycinate to oxide and the map redraws — more elemental magnesium per gram, worse tolerability, different cells open.

Grey is where the work is. Not a no — an engineering question with a known answer set: split the dose, change the salt, micronise, encapsulate, or move to a format that carries more.

The ICH concept of a design space, formalised for pharmaceutical development in 2005, applied to a category that has never had one.

Where the models sit
Read

Vision-language models over scanned consent orders, warning letters and monographs. Long-context extraction with span grounding, so every value returns with the page and line it came from. A field with no span does not persist.

Resolve

Embeddings over species, part and marker block the candidate set; a fine-tuned cross-encoder scores each pair. Below threshold nothing merges — the pair queues for review, and that judgement returns as a labelled example.

Decide

Symbolic. Feasibility, routing, permissibility and release run explicit rules against the resolved graph. Every output traces to the rule and the record behind it.

Retrieval, not recall

Nothing is answered from model weights. Generation is constrained to retrieved passages, and a drafted section containing an uncited factual sentence fails validation before anyone sees it.

Evaluated against known answers

Extraction and resolution are scored against a held-out set built by hand from filings and DSHEA-era records. We track precision on the cases that matter — a wrong merge, a missed objection — not aggregate accuracy.

Neural for perception, symbolic for judgement. A model reading a hundred thousand documents does work no team could. A model deciding whether an FDA objection applies to your ingredient does work no regulator would accept.

How we know what we know

Evidence, not assertion.

Every fact carries its source, its publication date, the date we fetched it and a staleness state. An uncited assertion is a defect, not a weaker answer.

Evidence tiers

Every capability, certification and compliance fact carries a tier. Routing weights by tier and never ranks on self-declaration alone.

A licences with endorsed categories, consent orders with product-wise capacity, compliance reports  ·  B certification registers, including revoked and suspended status  ·  C inspection classifications, import refusals, warning letters, recalls  ·  D websites and directories  ·  E patent filings, equipment import records, recruitment for specific process roles

Freshness

Stale regulatory data is worse than none, because people act on it. Each fact type carries a cadence and degrades against it.

Bitemporal by design. Every fact carries real-world validity and system belief time, so we can reconstruct what was known the day a dossier was filed.

Provenance

Where it came from, not just what it tests as.

Extract or finished bottle, same discipline. A clean assay tells you this lot is what it says — not where it was grown, what else the supplier ships, or whether the next lot matches.

What we hold on every material

  • Supplier of record — the entity, not a trading name. Screened against enforcement records, import refusals and certification status
  • Origin — growing region or production site, declared and recorded at intake
  • Lot chain — supplier lot to our intake lot to your batch, traceable in both directions
  • Specification history — what we accepted, when, and every change since, with the reason
  • Assay history — how this supplier's material has performed across lots and seasons, not a single certificate

Why history beats a certificate

One certificate is a snapshot from a party with an interest in the result. Ten lots is a pattern — and a pattern reveals drift, a poor season, or a supplier that changed something.

That history is what Proof reads when it sets the panel — which is why the panel changes lot to lot.

Every lot you buy adds to that record — which an audit cannot replicate and a certificate cannot express.

Where a claim depends on origin — a region, a cultivar, an organic scheme — we hold the certification and the chain of custody, and flag anything resting on a supplier declaration.

Audit and change control

What we commit to, in writing.

Factories are inspected and certified. Turia is audited by its customers, and audits its lines. Specifics are set in the quality agreement — different programmes carry different obligations.

  • Customer audits — of Turia, and of the line running your programme where the site agrees and notice allows
  • Quality agreement — signed before the first batch, defining responsibilities, specifications and how changes are handled
  • Change control — changes to a specification, method or line are logged with rationale and notified to you under the terms we agree
  • Deviation and investigation — documented and closed, and shared with you where your programme is affected
  • Reserve samples — held per lot under 21 CFR 111.83, for one year past the shelf-life date
  • Traceability — to line and raw-material lot, so a withdrawal can be scoped rather than guessed at
  • Complaints and adverse events — routed and investigated; serious adverse events reported within the statutory fifteen business days

For regulatory purposes the manufacturing facility remains the registered manufacturer. Turia is the specification holder and own-label distributor. Both are stated in the agreement, and both are audit-visible.

Before you ask

The questions we get.

What is your minimum order?

It depends on the format, the actives and the pack — anyone quoting a number before seeing your brief is guessing. What sets a minimum is the cost of putting a new product on a line, and we spread that across many programmes rather than one order.

Do you own your factories?

No. We own the specification, the analytical methods, the regulatory filings and an R&D bench. Manufacturing runs on qualified partner lines — so your product goes where that material class has run before, not to whichever equipment we happened to buy.

Then who is accountable if something goes wrong?

We are. If a batch is out of specification that is ours. If a line fails an audit mid-programme we move it — the specification is portable and a second source is scored before you need it. For regulatory purposes the facility remains the registered manufacturer; Turia is the specification holder and own-label distributor. Both are in the agreement.

How long does it take?

Roughly twelve weeks to first article where a platform exists, longer for a novel matrix — and we say which at the brief, not at month six. First commercial shipment adds packaging lead time, any accelerated stability needed, and transit.

Can you work with an ingredient I already buy?

Yes. Bring your supplier and we qualify them, or use ours. Either way it is tested on intake against a specification we hold — a supplier certificate is a reference, not a release.

Does my brief train your models?

No. Your formulation, your specification and anything you send stay yours and are not used to improve anything we sell to anyone else. What the system learns from is the public record and our own laboratory work — not your product.

Do I keep my formula?

Anything you bring is yours and stays yours. Where we develop something new, ownership is set in the agreement before work starts — better settled on day one than at renewal.

Why all-vegetarian?

A manufacturing control before it is a value: no cross-contact pathway, simpler cleaning, straightforward certification. It extends past the active — carriers and coatings carry animal-derived material more often than the ingredient does, and for a vegan claim we specify the fermentation medium too. It also means no collagen, fish oil or gelatin softgels.

Which markets can you supply?

The United States today. The EU and UK are on the roadmap, and we will say so plainly rather than take a programme we cannot document properly. Permissibility is checked component by component — sweeteners and colours included, because that is usually where a launch fails.

What testing do I get?

Identity and potency by validated methods, contaminant panels, microbiology and stability — method and reference standard named on the certificate, and the panel set per lot, not applied uniformly.

Can I audit you?

Yes, and we expect it — our laboratory, and the line running your programme where the site agrees and notice allows. A quality agreement is signed before the first batch. We will sign an NDA before you share anything, if you prefer.

What don't you make?

Nothing animal-derived — no collagen, fish oil, gelatin softgels, shellfish glucosamine or carmine. And no drug manufacture: OTC and prescription products need a different licence and a different facility. The line is the claim, not the molecule — the same magnesium is a supplement with a structure/function claim and a drug with a disease claim.

Can you supply just the extract, not a finished product?

Yes. We ship at four points — the active, the active engineered into a usable form, a premix, or the finished product. The specification, the qualification and the testing are the same at every one; only the last mile changes.

What does it cost to find out if my idea works?

The first-pass resolution is free. Bench work, stability and pilot production are quoted once we know what the programme needs — which we only know after the first pass.

What happens if a raw material becomes unavailable?

We hold a specification, not a supplier. Where a material has a single credible source we say so at the brief rather than after you have committed — that is a commercial risk you should price, not one we should hide.

Do you do stability and shelf-life studies?

Yes, accelerated and real-time, in the pack you will ship. Overage is calculated from that data rather than from convention — which is where most label-accuracy failures start.

I am not sure what I want yet.

Send the rough version. Half the briefs we see start as a competitor's label and a hunch — a perfectly good place to begin, and the eliminations are often more useful than the answer.
