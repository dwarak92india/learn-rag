Pick Your Trail · Community Strategy
International Travel Community

Turn every PYT trip into value for the next traveller.

Every completed journey creates first-hand knowledge. The opportunity is to keep that knowledge—and the people behind it—inside the PYT ecosystem.

One traveller's experience becomes another traveller's confidence.
The goal is not another social network for travellers.
It is a network that makes travellers want to help one another.
The three propositions

People. Knowledge. Belonging.

Three distinct community engines, each with its own behavioural mechanism and business role.

01 · People helping people

PYT Trail Mentors

Ask someone who's been there.

Behavioural principle — Mentorship + Social Recognition

People value guidance from someone who has personally experienced the problem they are facing. The mentor gains status and purpose when their experience helps another person.

“This person has actually been through what I'm about to do.”

Success precedent: Airbnb's host community. Experienced hosts have been used to help newer hosts navigate their journey—demonstrating how experience becomes more valuable when it helps someone else succeed.

02 · Knowledge that compounds

PYT Trail Map

Leave the trail better than you found it.

Behavioural principle — Reciprocity + Ownership + Social Recognition

A traveller discovers something, shares it, sees another person benefit, and feels that their contribution mattered. Over time, those contributions become a personal travel footprint.

“Someone else's contribution helped me. I can help the next person.”

Success precedent: Waze. Users contribute traffic conditions, incidents and map improvements to make the shared navigation experience better. Contribution systems and visible recognition reinforce participation. The PYT lesson is not to copy points—it is to make individual contributions visibly improve a shared experience.

03 · Belonging around a journey

PYT Travel Circles

Travel is better when you don't have to figure it out alone.

Behavioural principle — Belonging + Shared Identity + Group Quests

A shared destination creates interest. A shared problem creates conversation. Travellers participate more naturally when they feel they are among people facing similar questions.

“These are people who understand what I'm planning.”

Success precedent: Strava Clubs. Shared identity and collective goals give members a reason to return and interact beyond a one-time transaction.

A simple offline experiment

PYT Meet & Greet

“Japan Stories: Past Travellers Meet Future Travellers.”

Bring together a small group of returned travellers and upcoming travellers for an informal destination conversation. It is a low-complexity way to test the most important assumption behind the strategy: Do PYT travellers genuinely want to help and learn from one another?

The event isn't the strategy. It is a real-world experiment to validate the behaviour behind it.
